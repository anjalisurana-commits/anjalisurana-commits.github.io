Neel is categorizing research motivations, not research topics.



1.  Start with what could go wrong. (Addressing threat models)

2. Start with a proposal for building safe AI. (Agendas to build safe AGI)

3. Work on things that seem useful regardless of how AI develops. (Robustly good approaches)

4. Figure out what we're confused about first. (De-confusion)

5. Build the community of people who can solve the problem. (Field-building)

two researchers can agree about the importance of AI alignment while having very different ideas about how to make progress.

There's one more important layer: he thinks researchers' choices are influenced by their beliefs about timelines, takeoff speed, whether AGI will resemble current systems, and how difficult alignment will be. That's why he ends the article with those considerations.

  
And the differences often come down to what each researcher believes we already know.
